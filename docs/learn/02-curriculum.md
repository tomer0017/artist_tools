# 02 — Curriculum specification (proposal, for approval)

A pencil-first drawing curriculum of 46 exercises in 11 units, plus a separate
introductory colour module of 7 exercises. The order follows the research in
[01-research.md](01-research.md): observation from the first session, short
technique drills always followed by drawing something real, value before
colour, big shapes before detail. Nothing here is timed, nothing is mandatory,
and no unit is a prerequisite: Learn recommends an order and lets people
(and teachers) choose.

## How to read an exercise

Each exercise has a **stable ID** (never reused or renumbered; display order is
separate), an English and a Hebrew title, and these fields:

- **Objective** — what the exercise trains.
- **Materials** — what to have on the table.
- **Student** — the short instruction on the card (one or two sentences;
  Hebrew versions are written in implementation and reviewed by a native
  speaker, gender-neutral as in the rest of the app).
- **Guide** — optional expanded guidance for teachers and independent
  learners (behind "More help").
- **Reference** — what image, if any, the exercise needs.
- **Mistake → fix** — one common mistake and a practical correction.
- **Tool** — an optional Studio Companion shortcut.
- **Worksheet** — whether a printable worksheet is useful.
- **Success** — a simple, observable criterion the learner can check alone.

Success criteria describe what the drawing shows, never a score. Where a
criterion says "when overlaid", the learner photographs the drawing and
checks it in Compare & Correct; that is optional feedback, not a test.

Abbreviations for tools: **Measure** (Measure & Plan), **Study** (Study &
Simplify), **Color** (Color & Mixing), **Grid** (Create a Grid), **Compare**
(Compare & Correct), **Worksheets** (Prepare Worksheets).

## Sequence at a glance

| Unit | Focus | Exercises | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 Start | A first drawing from life | 1 | Success before drills; a baseline to revisit |
| 1 Lines | Hand control: lines, curves, spirals, ellipses | 4 | Each drill ends in a real object |
| 2 Shapes | Silhouettes and simple 3-D forms | 3 | |
| 3 Observation | Contour, negative space, angles, relative measurement, alignment, block-in | 7 | The core of accurate drawing (research: perception, not the hand, is the main source of error) |
| 4 Value | Value scales, transitions, seeing and planning values | 4 | |
| 5 Light | Light and shadow on form; cast shadows; still life in light | 4 | |
| 6 Perspective | One-, two- and introductory three-point | 5 | Perspective as a way to check observation |
| 7 Copying | Grid method, measured copy, the eye | 4 | The grid is a transfer and checking aid, taught beside measuring |
| 8 Head | Proportions, turning the head, features, portrait | 5 | Proportions are averages: measure the real face |
| 9 Figure | Gesture, proportions, a simple mannequin, hands | 5 | |
| 10 Subjects | Animals, landscape, composition, a complete drawing | 4 | |
| C Colour | Colour basics to a small complete painting | 7 | Separate module; pencil first is recommended, never required |

A suggested rhythm (not enforced): after each unit, one or two challenges
from [03-challenges.md](03-challenges.md) that use the same skill, mixing
earlier skills back in. Mixing subjects helps people learn to tell things
apart; for drawing practice itself it is plausible but untested, and nothing
supports promising results by a date (01 §2).

## Unit 0 — Start drawing

**D-START-01 · A first drawing from life · רישום ראשון מהתבוננות**
- Objective: draw a real object from observation before any drill, and keep it as a baseline to revisit.
- Materials: HB pencil, A4 paper, a mug or cup.
- Student: "Put a mug in front of you. Look at it more than at the paper, and draw what you see."
- Guide: Encourage slow looking; no erasing yet. Keep the drawing and repeat this exercise after Units 3 and 5 to see the change for yourself (no grading).
- Reference: a real object; a provided photo of a mug for anyone without one.
- Mistake → fix: drawing the idea of a mug (a round opening seen from above) → compare the opening's height with its width: from the side it is a flat ellipse.
- Tool: Compare (optional, with a photo of the same mug).
- Worksheet: no.
- Success: the outline, the opening and the handle are all there, in roughly the right places.

## Unit 1 — Lines and hand control

**D-LINE-01 · Confident straight lines · קווים ישרים בתנועה בטוחה**
- Objective: draw a straight line between two points in one confident stroke ("ghosting" the movement first).
- Materials: HB or 2B pencil, paper.
- Student: "Mark two dots. Move your hand over the path a few times without touching, then draw it in one stroke."
- Guide: Long lines come from the elbow and shoulder, not the wrist; turn the paper to a comfortable angle. Missing the dot is fine; hesitating is what to avoid.
- Reference: none (worksheet of dot pairs).
- Mistake → fix: slow, hairy lines made of many short strokes → one stroke at an even speed, then the next.
- Tool: none.
- Worksheet: yes — dot pairs at varied lengths and angles.
- Success: most lines are single strokes that start and end near their dots.

**D-LINE-02 · Curves, S-curves and spirals · עקומות, קווי S וספירלות**
- Objective: control curvature and changes of direction.
- Materials: pencil, paper.
- Student: "Connect three dots with one smooth curve. Then draw spirals from the outside in, keeping the gaps even."
- Guide: Ghost the whole curve before drawing; curves with a change of direction (S-curves) are the backbone of gesture later.
- Reference: none (worksheet).
- Mistake → fix: flat spots and corners in a curve → draw a little faster with the whole arm.
- Tool: none.
- Worksheet: yes — three-dot sets and spiral starts.
- Success: curves pass near their dots without kinks; spiral gaps look roughly even.

**D-LINE-03 · Ellipses · אליפסות**
- Objective: draw ellipses of different openness with round, symmetrical ends — the basis of cups, cylinders and wheels.
- Materials: pencil, paper.
- Student: "Draw an ellipse in each printed box: go around two or three times lightly, then once firmly. Keep the ends round."
- Guide: An ellipse's openness (its "degree") is how a circle looks when tilted; the short axis matters later for perspective.
- Reference: none (worksheet).
- Mistake → fix: pointed ends, like a lemon → keep the hand moving through the ends without stopping.
- Tool: none.
- Worksheet: yes — boxes of different proportions with their axes.
- Success: ellipses fill their boxes with rounded, roughly symmetrical ends.

**D-LINE-04 · Lines and ellipses on a real object · קווים ואליפסות על חפץ אמיתי**
- Objective: apply line and ellipse control to an object straight away.
- Materials: pencil, paper, a glass or jar.
- Student: "Draw a glass from life: straight sides, an ellipse for the rim and a curve for the base."
- Guide: Below eye level, the base ellipse is a little more open than the rim.
- Reference: a real object (provided photo as fallback).
- Mistake → fix: a flat line for the base → the base is part of an ellipse too.
- Tool: Measure (optional, height to width on a photo).
- Worksheet: no.
- Success: rim and base curve consistently; the sides are straight.

## Unit 2 — Shapes and simple forms

**D-SHAPE-01 · Seeing big shapes · לראות צורות גדולות**
- Objective: reduce objects to their overall flat shape (silhouette) before any detail.
- Materials: soft pencil, paper, three objects.
- Student: "Draw three objects only as their outer shape, then fill each shape in dark."
- Guide: A filled silhouette shows proportion errors that outlines hide.
- Reference: real objects, or a provided photo set.
- Mistake → fix: starting with details → squint until details disappear and draw what is left.
- Tool: Study (Values, 2–3 levels) to see the silhouettes in a photo.
- Worksheet: no.
- Success: each silhouette is recognisable without any inner detail.

**D-SHAPE-02 · Cube, cylinder, sphere, cone · קובייה, גליל, כדור וחרוט**
- Objective: draw basic 3-D forms with their structure visible.
- Materials: pencil, paper; optional real box, can and ball.
- Student: "Draw each form as if it were glass, showing the hidden edges lightly."
- Guide: Cylinders are two ellipses joined by straight sides; the ellipse further from eye level is more open.
- Reference: none, or simple objects.
- Mistake → fix: a cylinder with flat ends → both ends are ellipses.
- Tool: none.
- Worksheet: optional — construction guides.
- Success: every form reads as 3-D, with ellipses consistent with its axis.

**D-SHAPE-03 · Objects as combined forms · חפצים כצירוף של גופים**
- Objective: build everyday objects from simple forms before refining the outline.
- Materials: pencil, paper, a bottle and a teapot or lamp.
- Student: "Stack simple forms to build a bottle and a teapot, then refine the outline over them."
- Guide: Align the forms on one central axis; erase the construction only at the end, if at all.
- Reference: real objects (provided photos as fallback).
- Mistake → fix: forms that float apart → share one axis and touch where the object joins.
- Tool: Measure (optional).
- Success: the construction is visible and the object's proportions are believable.

## Unit 3 — Observation, angles and proportion

**D-OBS-01 · Slow contour · קו מתאר איטי**
- Objective: train attention to edges by moving eye and hand together.
- Materials: pencil, paper; your hand or a plant.
- Student: "Draw the edge of your hand very slowly, eyes mostly on the hand. Look at the paper only to restart."
- Guide: A traditional contour exercise (Nicolaides), offered as an attention warm-up: it is untested as a way to draw more accurately (01 §2). Distorted results are expected and fine; the point is looking.
- Reference: real subject.
- Mistake → fix: drawing faster than the eye moves → let the pencil follow the eye, not run ahead.
- Tool: none.
- Worksheet: no.
- Success: the line records many small changes of direction along the edge (accuracy is not the goal here).

**D-OBS-02 · Negative space · חלל שלילי**
- Objective: draw the shapes around and between parts of an object to get its proportions right.
- Materials: pencil, paper, a chair or a plant.
- Student: "Draw only the empty shapes between the legs and bars of a chair. The chair appears by itself."
- Guide: Negative shapes have no name, so they are drawn as seen rather than as known.
- Reference: real subject; provided photo as fallback.
- Mistake → fix: drawing the parts from memory → compare the widths and heights of the gaps instead.
- Tool: Study (Values, 2 levels) makes the empty shapes obvious in a photo.
- Worksheet: optional — a high-contrast reference.
- Success: the gaps have believable sizes, and the chair's proportions read correctly.

**D-OBS-03 · Measuring angles · מדידת זוויות**
- Objective: judge angles against vertical and horizontal and transfer them.
- Materials: pencil, paper, a subject with clear edges.
- Student: "Hold the pencil at arm's length along an edge, keep that angle, and lay it on your paper."
- Guide: Keep the pencil flat in an imaginary window parallel to your face, one eye closed.
- Reference: real subject or a provided photo.
- Mistake → fix: tilting the pencil toward you → keep it in the window plane.
- Tool: Measure (angles on a photo); Compare (optional check).
- Worksheet: no.
- Success: the main angles in the drawing match the subject when checked.

**D-OBS-04 · Relative measurement · מדידה יחסית בעזרת העיפרון**
- Objective: compare proportions against one chosen unit.
- Materials: pencil, paper, a bottle or any object.
- Student: "Pick one small part as your unit and measure everything else in units, arm straight and locked."
- Guide: Measure only the big relationships; check one with another (height against width). Glance between subject and drawing often: frequent short checks go with more accurate drawings (01 §1).
- Reference: real subject or a provided photo.
- Mistake → fix: bending the arm between measurements → lock the elbow so the scale stays the same.
- Tool: Measure (real proportions on a photo).
- Worksheet: no.
- Success: the main height-to-width relationships match when checked with Measure or Compare.

**D-OBS-05 · Alignments · קווי יישור אנכיים ואופקיים**
- Objective: place points by what lines up vertically and horizontally.
- Materials: pencil, paper.
- Student: "Find what lines up straight down or straight across in the subject and draw light guide lines."
- Guide: Every new point should be checked against at least two others.
- Reference: real subject or a provided photo.
- Mistake → fix: placing parts one by one → check each against an alignment.
- Tool: Measure (guide lines on a photo).
- Worksheet: optional — reference with a few alignments marked.
- Success: key points sit on the right vertical and horizontal relations.

**D-OBS-06 · Block-in with straight lines · חלוקה ראשונית בקווים ישרים**
- Objective: start any complex shape with a few straight lines, curves last.
- Materials: pencil, paper.
- Student: "Enclose the subject in four to eight straight lines, then cut smaller shapes inside them. Curves come last."
- Guide: The atelier block-in (Bargue tradition). Nothing smaller than a thumb until the big shape is right. When a contour is complex, copy it as short, simple segments, one at a time, as experienced drawers do (01 §1).
- Reference: provided reference (a shoe, a bird, a cast).
- Mistake → fix: details too early → stay with the big shape until it matches.
- Tool: Compare (photograph the block-in and overlay it).
- Worksheet: yes — reference and an empty frame of the same size.
- Success: when overlaid, the block-in's outer shape matches the reference's proportions.

**D-OBS-07 · Placing a still life · מיקום קבוצת חפצים בדף**
- Objective: combine measuring, angles and negative space for two or three objects, and plan placement on the page.
- Materials: pencil, paper, two or three objects.
- Student: "Mark the top, bottom and sides of the whole group first, then each object's place, then draw."
- Guide: Plan the group's size so it fits the page with some space around it.
- Reference: real objects.
- Mistake → fix: drawing too small or off the page → mark the extremes before anything else.
- Tool: Measure; Compare.
- Worksheet: optional.
- Success: the group fits the page as planned and the objects' relative sizes read correctly.

## Unit 4 — Value

**D-VAL-01 · Value scale · סולם ערכים**
- Objective: make five, then nine, even steps from paper white to the darkest dark.
- Materials: HB, 2B and 4B (or 6B) pencils, paper.
- Student: "Fill the boxes from white to your darkest dark in even steps."
- Guide: Value is lightness alone (Munsell's value scale is the classic reference). Five steps are enough to start; squint to compare.
- Reference: none (worksheet).
- Mistake → fix: middle steps that look alike and darks that stay grey → build darks in layers with a softer pencil; press less in the lights.
- Tool: none.
- Worksheet: yes — 5- and 9-step strips.
- Success: neighbouring steps are distinguishable and the steps look even.

**D-VAL-02 · Smooth transitions and hatching · מעברים רכים וקווקוו**
- Objective: control transitions, both smooth and hatched.
- Materials: pencils, paper.
- Student: "Shade a strip from dark to light with no visible steps. Then do it again with hatching lines."
- Guide: Light layers, a consistent stroke direction; cross-hatching for deeper darks.
- Reference: none (worksheet).
- Mistake → fix: blotches from uneven pressure → more, lighter layers.
- Tool: none.
- Worksheet: yes.
- Success: no visible jumps along either strip.

**D-VAL-03 · Seeing three values · לראות שלושה ערכים**
- Objective: simplify a picture into light, middle and dark shapes.
- Materials: pencils, paper.
- Student: "Simplify the photo to three values in Study & Simplify, then draw those three shapes with three tones."
- Guide: Merge small shapes into the big shape they belong to.
- Reference: provided photo or the learner's own.
- Mistake → fix: copying every small spot → squint and keep only big shapes.
- Tool: Study (Values, 3 levels).
- Worksheet: yes — the 3-value version and an outline.
- Success: the drawing reads clearly with three tones only.

**D-VAL-04 · Value thumbnails · סקיצות ערכים קטנות**
- Objective: plan a picture in three or four values in palm-sized sketches.
- Materials: pencil, paper.
- Student: "Make two small sketches of the same scene with only three or four values. Keep the clearer one."
- Guide: This is a planning step that carries into composition and painting.
- Reference: provided photo or own.
- Mistake → fix: thumbnails that grow and get detailed → keep them small.
- Tool: Study (Values).
- Worksheet: optional.
- Success: the subject separates clearly from its background by value.

## Unit 5 — Light and form

**D-LIGHT-01 · Light on a sphere · אור על כדור**
- Objective: render light, halftone, core shadow, reflected light, cast shadow and contact shadow on a sphere under one light.
- Materials: a ball, a lamp (one light source), pencils.
- Student: "Light a ball from one side. Draw it, find where light turns to shadow, mark the cast shadow, then shade."
- Guide: Keep reflected light within the shadow family: darker than any lit area.
- Reference: real setup; provided photo as fallback.
- Mistake → fix: reflected light as bright as the light side → darken it until the shadow side reads as one shape.
- Tool: Study (grayscale of a photo of the setup, optional).
- Worksheet: optional — circle and light direction.
- Success: the light and shadow sides separate clearly; core and cast shadows are visible.

**D-LIGHT-02 · Planes: box and cylinder · מישורים: קופסה וגליל**
- Objective: show form through planes facing toward or away from the light.
- Materials: a box, a can, a lamp, pencils.
- Student: "Shade a box and a can in one light: each face of the box one value; the can turning gradually."
- Guide: Edges come from value changes, not outlines.
- Reference: real setup.
- Mistake → fix: outlining everything in dark lines → let neighbouring values make the edges.
- Tool: none.
- Worksheet: no.
- Success: the box's three visible faces have three distinct values matching the light.

**D-LIGHT-03 · Cast shadows · צלים מוטלים**
- Objective: the shape and direction of cast shadows, and how their edges soften with distance.
- Materials: a cup, a lamp, pencils.
- Student: "Move the lamp and draw how the cup's shadow changes length and direction."
- Guide: A cast shadow is darkest and sharpest where it touches the object.
- Reference: real setup.
- Mistake → fix: shadows equally dark with hard edges everywhere → darker and sharper near the object, softer further away.
- Tool: none.
- Worksheet: no.
- Success: shadows point away from the light and soften with distance.

**D-LIGHT-04 · Still life in light · טבע דומם באור**
- Objective: combine form, value and cast shadows on two or three simple objects from life.
- Materials: an egg, a cup and a fruit; one lamp; pencils.
- Student: "Block in two or three objects under one lamp, map the shadow shapes, then shade."
- Guide: Work the whole drawing at once rather than finishing one object at a time.
- Reference: real setup.
- Mistake → fix: light from different directions on different objects → one light, checked on every object.
- Tool: Study (values of a photo of the setup); Compare.
- Worksheet: optional.
- Success: one consistent light direction across all objects.

## Unit 6 — Perspective

**D-PERSP-01 · Eye level and one-point perspective · קו האופק ונקודת מגוז אחת**
- Objective: the horizon (eye level), a vanishing point, receding parallel lines.
- Materials: pencil, ruler (optional), paper.
- Student: "Draw a road or a corridor: a horizon line, one vanishing point, every receding edge heading to it."
- Guide: The horizon is always at the viewer's eye height.
- Reference: none, or a provided photo.
- Mistake → fix: a horizon placed anywhere → it is your eye level.
- Tool: none.
- Worksheet: yes — horizon and vanishing point guides.
- Success: all receding edges meet at one point on the horizon.

**D-PERSP-02 · Boxes in two-point perspective · קופסאות בשתי נקודות מגוז**
- Objective: boxes above, on and below eye level with two vanishing points.
- Materials: pencil, ruler (optional), paper.
- Student: "Draw boxes above, on and below the horizon, with two vanishing points far apart."
- Guide: Vanishing points too close together distort the boxes; place them wide, even off the page.
- Reference: none.
- Mistake → fix: vanishing points close together → spread them out.
- Tool: none.
- Worksheet: yes.
- Success: verticals stay vertical and receding edges converge correctly.

**D-PERSP-03 · Circles in perspective · מעגלים בפרספקטיבה**
- Objective: ellipses for cups, cylinders and wheels, aligned with their axis.
- Materials: pencil, paper, cups.
- Student: "Draw cups at different heights: their ellipses open up the further they are from eye level."
- Guide: The ellipse's short axis lines up with the cylinder's axis.
- Reference: real objects.
- Mistake → fix: a tilted ellipse on an upright cup → the short axis follows the cup's axis.
- Tool: none.
- Worksheet: optional.
- Success: ellipse openness changes consistently with height.

**D-PERSP-04 · Introducing three-point perspective · היכרות עם שלוש נקודות מגוז**
- Objective: verticals converging to a third point when looking up or down.
- Materials: pencil, ruler (optional), paper.
- Student: "Draw a tall building seen from below: its verticals meet at a point high above."
- Guide: An introduction only; most observational drawing needs one- or two-point.
- Reference: a provided photo.
- Mistake → fix: verticals kept parallel while the horizontals converge → let them meet above.
- Tool: none.
- Worksheet: yes — three vanishing points.
- Success: verticals converge consistently and the building feels tall.

**D-PERSP-05 · Perspective from observation · פרספקטיבה מהתבוננות**
- Objective: use perspective to check a drawing of a real room or street.
- Materials: pencil, paper.
- Student: "Draw a corner of a room or a street. Find your eye level first, then check angles with your pencil."
- Guide: Perspective explains what you measure; measurement still leads.
- Reference: real scene or own photo.
- Mistake → fix: drawing by rules alone → measure the angles, then use perspective to check them.
- Tool: Measure (angles on a photo); Compare.
- Worksheet: no.
- Success: the main edges agree with the eye level and the measured angles.

## Unit 7 — Copying, grids and the eye

**D-COPY-01 · The grid method · שיטת הרשת**
- Objective: copy an image accurately square by square; understand proportional transfer.
- Materials: pencil, the printed worksheets, ruler.
- Student: "Copy one square at a time: look at where the edges enter and leave each square."
- Guide: Start with 4 × 4, go finer for detail. The grid is a transfer and checking aid; measuring (Unit 3) remains the skill.
- Reference: provided reference or own photo.
- Mistake → fix: drawing the subject instead of the square → treat each square as an abstract shape.
- Tool: Grid; Worksheets.
- Worksheet: yes — reference with grid, matching blank grid.
- Success: when overlaid, the main contours fall in the right squares.

**D-COPY-02 · A measured copy without a grid · העתקה מדויקת בלי רשת**
- Objective: copy a simple image using block-in and measurement, then check and correct.
- Materials: pencil, the printed reference, paper.
- Student: "Copy the reference at the same size with a straight-line block-in, then check it and correct."
- Guide: Same-size copying (as in the Bargue plates) makes checking direct.
- Reference: a provided simple reference.
- Mistake → fix: correcting by feel → compare, find the largest error, fix that first.
- Tool: Compare.
- Worksheet: yes — reference and an empty frame of the same size.
- Success: when overlaid, the main shapes align closely after one round of correction.

**D-COPY-03 · Constructing an eye · בניית עין**
- Objective: the eye as a form: the eyeball, the thickness of the lids, the iris partly under the upper lid, the highlight, the tear duct, the lashes' direction.
- Materials: pencil, paper.
- Student: "Start with a ball, wrap the lids around it, place the iris partly under the upper lid, then the pupil and highlight."
- Guide: The upper lid casts a shadow on the eyeball; lashes curve and clump.
- Reference: provided eye photos (front and three-quarter).
- Mistake → fix: the whole iris showing (a staring look) → let the upper lid cover its top.
- Tool: Worksheets.
- Worksheet: yes — reference, outline, grid.
- Success: the eye reads as a form, with lid thickness and an iris partly under the lid.

**D-COPY-04 · An eye in values · עין בערכים**
- Objective: render the constructed eye with values.
- Materials: pencils, the outline worksheet.
- Student: "On the outline sheet, shade the eye in three values first, then refine the transitions."
- Guide: The white of the eye is shaded; only the highlight is paper white.
- Reference: same as D-COPY-03.
- Mistake → fix: a paper-white eyeball → tone it; save pure white for the highlight.
- Tool: Study (Values, 3–5 levels).
- Worksheet: yes — outline and value version.
- Success: the eyeball is shaded and the highlight is the brightest spot.

## Unit 8 — The head and the portrait

**D-HEAD-01 · Proportions of the head, front view · פרופורציות הראש במבט חזיתי**
- Objective: average facial proportions as a first map, adjusted to the real person.
- Materials: pencil, paper, a mirror or photo.
- Student: "Draw an oval, a centre line and an eye line about halfway down; place the nose and mouth; then compare with a real face."
- Guide: These are averages. The classical canons rarely fit real faces, and knowing the "eyes halfway" average only partly fixes the habit of drawing them too high (01 §3). Measure the person in front of you and use the map only to check.
- Reference: provided front-view photo.
- Mistake → fix: eyes too high and a small forehead → eyes are usually near the middle of the head's height.
- Tool: Measure; Worksheets.
- Worksheet: yes — proportion guide.
- Success: features follow the guide lines and the face looks plausible.

**D-HEAD-02 · The head turning · הראש בסיבוב: שלושה רבעים ופרופיל**
- Objective: the head as a ball and jaw turning in space (Loomis-style construction).
- Materials: pencil, paper.
- Student: "Draw the head as a ball with a flat side, add the jaw and neck, then turn it to three-quarter and profile."
- Guide: The centre line curves with the turn; the far eye narrows.
- Reference: provided three-quarter and profile photos.
- Mistake → fix: features staying frontal while the head turns → wrap them along the curved centre line.
- Tool: none.
- Worksheet: optional.
- Success: the features wrap around the head consistently with the turn.

**D-HEAD-03 · Features: nose, mouth, ear · תווי פנים: אף, פה ואוזן**
- Objective: simple plane structures for each feature.
- Materials: pencils, paper.
- Student: "Draw each feature as simple planes before details: the nose as a wedge, the lips as three masses, the ear as a C-shape."
- Guide: Describe features with light and shadow shapes rather than outlines.
- Reference: provided photos of features.
- Mistake → fix: hard outlines around nostrils and lips → use shadow shapes instead.
- Tool: Study (values).
- Worksheet: optional.
- Success: features read through value shapes rather than outlines.

**D-HEAD-04 · Portrait from a photo · דיוקן מתצלום**
- Objective: combine block-in, measurement and values in a portrait.
- Materials: pencils, worksheets.
- Student: "Block in the head, place the features with alignments, shade in three values, then refine."
- Guide: Use the grid version only if needed; check with Compare before details.
- Reference: provided portrait or own photo (with the sitter's consent).
- Mistake → fix: starting with an eye and building outwards → the head's shape first.
- Tool: Measure; Study; Grid; Compare; Worksheets.
- Worksheet: yes — reference, outline, grid, values.
- Success: when overlaid, the main features align closely.

**D-HEAD-05 · Portrait from life · דיוקן מהחיים**
- Objective: a portrait of a sitting person from observation.
- Materials: pencils, paper; a willing sitter.
- Student: "Draw someone sitting still: start big and simple, measure relatively, add values last."
- Guide: Short sessions with breaks; keep the light steady.
- Reference: real sitter.
- Mistake → fix: chasing detail while the sitter moves → stay with big shapes and light.
- Tool: none.
- Worksheet: no.
- Success: the sitter's head shape and feature placement are recognisable.

## Unit 9 — The figure

**D-FIG-01 · Gesture · רישום תנועה**
- Objective: capture movement and the line of action with a few lines.
- Materials: pencil, paper.
- Student: "Draw people or poses with a few flowing lines that show the movement. Many small drawings, no details."
- Guide: The gesture tradition (Nicolaides, Vilppu). Small drawings keep the focus on movement; no timer is needed.
- Reference: real people, or provided pose photos.
- Mistake → fix: stiff outlines → start with one curve through the whole body.
- Tool: none.
- Worksheet: no.
- Success: each drawing shows the pose's direction and balance.

**D-FIG-02 · Proportions of the figure · פרופורציות הגוף**
- Objective: average adult proportions in head lengths, and the main landmarks.
- Materials: pencil, paper.
- Student: "Draw a standing figure measured in head lengths; mark chin, chest, navel, hips, knees and feet."
- Guide: Averages only: about 7.5 heads for adults (8 is the idealised figure), and children grow from about 5.6 heads at age 2 to about 7.5 at 15 (01 §3). Measure the real figure; the head count is a check.
- Reference: provided standing-figure photo.
- Mistake → fix: legs too short → for adults, the hips are near half the total height.
- Tool: Measure; Worksheets.
- Worksheet: yes — head-unit guide.
- Success: the landmarks fall near the expected head units.

**D-FIG-03 · A simple mannequin · בובת ציור פשוטה**
- Objective: ribcage, pelvis, spine and limbs as simple forms on a gesture.
- Materials: pencil, paper.
- Student: "Over a gesture line, build the ribcage as an egg, the pelvis as a bowl, and the limbs as cylinders."
- Guide: Ribcage and pelvis tilt independently; that is where most of the pose lives.
- Reference: provided pose photos.
- Mistake → fix: ribcage and pelvis level and stacked → tilt them with the pose.
- Tool: none.
- Worksheet: no.
- Success: the mannequin holds the pose with believable proportions.

**D-FIG-04 · Hands · ידיים**
- Objective: the hand's structure: the palm as a box, finger segments, the thumb's base.
- Materials: pencil, paper, your free hand.
- Student: "Draw your free hand: the palm as a box first, then the finger blocks, then the outline."
- Guide: Finger segments shorten towards the tips; the knuckles form a curve.
- Reference: own hand; provided hand photos.
- Mistake → fix: fingers as equal sausages → segments that shorten towards the tips.
- Tool: none.
- Worksheet: no.
- Success: palm and fingers have believable proportions and joints.

**D-FIG-05 · A figure from life or a photo · דמות מהחיים או מתצלום**
- Objective: gesture, then structure, then light, in one drawing.
- Materials: pencils, paper.
- Student: "Start with the gesture, build the mannequin over it, then add light and shadow."
- Guide: Keep the gesture visible as long as possible.
- Reference: real person or provided photo.
- Mistake → fix: losing the movement while adding anatomy → recheck against the first gesture line.
- Tool: Measure; Compare.
- Worksheet: optional.
- Success: the pose and proportions read clearly, with one light direction.

## Unit 10 — Subjects and composition

**D-SUBJ-01 · Animals from simple shapes · בעלי חיים מצורות פשוטות**
- Objective: build animals from a gesture and three masses.
- Materials: pencil, paper.
- Student: "Draw a cat or a dog: a gesture line, three masses (head, chest, hips), simple legs, then the outline."
- Guide: Most four-legged animals share this plan; proportions change by species.
- Reference: provided animal photos, or a pet.
- Mistake → fix: starting with the face → the body's masses first.
- Tool: Study.
- Worksheet: optional.
- Success: the animal's pose and proportions of its masses read correctly.

**D-SUBJ-02 · Landscape: big shapes and depth · נוף: צורות גדולות ועומק**
- Objective: simplify a landscape into a few planes and show depth (less contrast and lighter values with distance).
- Materials: pencils, paper.
- Student: "Divide the scene into foreground, middle ground, background and sky; give each its own value."
- Guide: Atmospheric perspective: distant shapes lose contrast and detail.
- Reference: own photo or a provided landscape.
- Mistake → fix: equal detail everywhere → keep detail and contrast for the foreground.
- Tool: Study (Values).
- Worksheet: optional.
- Success: the planes separate clearly and recede.

**D-SUBJ-03 · Composition in thumbnails · קומפוזיציה בסקיצות קטנות**
- Objective: try arrangements before committing: focal point, value structure, placement.
- Materials: pencil, paper.
- Student: "Make three small arrangements of one subject and choose the one with the clearest centre of interest."
- Guide: Guidelines such as the rule of thirds are options, not laws.
- Reference: own photo or provided.
- Mistake → fix: the subject centred and small by default → try off-centre and larger placements.
- Tool: Study (Values).
- Worksheet: no.
- Success: one thumbnail has a clear focal point made by contrast and placement.

**D-SUBJ-04 · A complete drawing · רישום שלם מההתחלה עד הסוף**
- Objective: take a drawing from reference to finish with the whole process.
- Materials: pencils, paper, worksheets.
- Student: "Thumbnail, block-in, values, details: one complete drawing of a subject you choose."
- Guide: Use any earlier exercise as a step when needed.
- Reference: own photo or provided.
- Mistake → fix: rushing to details → a full value pass before any detail.
- Tool: Worksheets; Study; Measure; Compare.
- Worksheet: yes.
- Success: a planned composition, believable proportions and consistent light.

## Colour module (separate; pencil first recommended, never required)

**C-COL-01 · Hue, value and chroma · גוון, ערך ורוויה**
- Objective: the three dimensions of colour, and why value carries the form.
- Materials: paints (acrylic, gouache or watercolour), brush, paper.
- Student: "Paint one colour from light to dark, then swatches of different colours that look equally light."
- Guide: Check value with a grayscale photo of the swatches.
- Reference: none.
- Mistake → fix: judging lightness by hue → check in grayscale.
- Tool: Color; Study (grayscale).
- Worksheet: optional — swatch boxes.
- Success: swatches meant to share a value look alike in grayscale.

**C-COL-02 · Mixing with a limited palette · ערבוב בפלטה מצומצמת**
- Objective: mix a chart from a few colours and match a target colour.
- Materials: a warm and a cool version of each primary, white, palette, paper.
- Student: "Mix a small chart from your colours, then mix one colour from a photo as closely as you can."
- Guide: Fewer tubes means more control over mixtures.
- Reference: a provided or own photo.
- Mistake → fix: black to darken everything → darken with darker or complementary colours.
- Tool: Color (target colour and mixing guidance).
- Worksheet: optional — chart boxes.
- Success: a full chart, and one target matched by eye.

**C-COL-03 · Preparing a canvas · הכנת קנבס**
- Objective: choose size and ratio, prime the surface, optionally tone it.
- Materials: canvas or board, gesso, brush.
- Student: "Choose a canvas that matches your reference's ratio, prime it evenly, and tone it if you like."
- Guide: A mid-value toned ground makes judging lights and darks easier.
- Reference: none.
- Mistake → fix: a ratio that does not match the reference → plan the ratio first.
- Tool: Grid (canvas size and ratio).
- Worksheet: no.
- Success: an evenly primed surface in the planned ratio.

**C-COL-04 · Transferring the drawing · העברת הרישום לקנבס**
- Objective: transfer a drawing to the canvas accurately.
- Materials: canvas, pencil or thin paint, ruler.
- Student: "Draw a light grid on the canvas matching your printed grid, and transfer square by square."
- Guide: Keep transfer lines light so they do not muddy the paint.
- Reference: own reference with its worksheets.
- Mistake → fix: heavy graphite lines → light lines, or redraw them in thin paint.
- Tool: Grid; Measure; Worksheets.
- Worksheet: yes — reference with grid, blank grid.
- Success: the main contours are in the right place on the canvas.

**C-COL-05 · Simplifying into value and colour groups · פישוט לקבוצות ערך וצבע**
- Objective: plan a painting with three to five values and a few colour groups.
- Materials: paints, small paper or board.
- Student: "Simplify the reference to three to five values and a few colours, and paint a small study."
- Guide: The study is a plan, not a finished painting.
- Reference: own or provided photo.
- Mistake → fix: too many small colour shapes → merge them into the big groups.
- Tool: Study (Values; Color and Painter modes).
- Worksheet: optional — value version.
- Success: the study reads clearly at a distance.

**C-COL-06 · Block-in and layers · מסות ראשונות ושכבות**
- Objective: big shapes first in thin paint, then refine towards detail in layers.
- Materials: paints, large and small brushes, the prepared canvas.
- Student: "Cover the canvas with big shapes in thin paint first; refine and add detail in later layers."
- Guide: With acrylics, let layers dry; with oils, follow "fat over lean".
- Reference: own reference.
- Mistake → fix: detail before the canvas is covered → big shapes first, everywhere.
- Tool: Compare (check progress against the reference).
- Worksheet: no.
- Success: the whole canvas is covered with big shapes before any detail.

**C-COL-07 · A small complete painting · ציור קטן ושלם**
- Objective: one small painting from reference to finish.
- Materials: paints, canvas or board.
- Student: "Plan, transfer, block in and finish one small painting."
- Guide: Reuse the earlier colour exercises as steps.
- Reference: own or provided.
- Mistake → fix: losing the value plan while adding colour → check the painting in grayscale.
- Tool: Worksheets; Grid; Study; Color; Compare.
- Worksheet: yes.
- Success: a finished small painting whose value structure matches its plan.
