# 02 — Curriculum specification (proposal, for approval)

**Revised 2026-11-18 after the owner's first review (80/20).** The first
proposal had 46 pencil exercises in 11 units (commit `53a53ed`). It was
checked against the research in [01-research.md](01-research.md):

- **What the research says matters most:** most drawing errors come from how
  the subject is seen and checked, not from hand control (01 §1).
- **What v1 keeps:** the exercises that train looking, measuring and checking,
  and values. Length is cut.

The result:

- **12 core exercises.** The smallest set that covers the practical skills of
  observational drawing:
  - big shapes first;
  - negative space;
  - angles and alignments;
  - relative measurement;
  - block-in;
  - a checked copy;
  - values and light;
  - a still life and a portrait.
- **8 next steps:** grid, perspective, figure, hands, the eye, the turning
  head, landscape, and a complete drawing.
- **The colour module (7 exercises):** separate, unchanged.
- **26 of the original 46 are no longer separate exercises:**
  - 18 are merged into the 20 above;
  - 8 are deferred: their subjects are covered by challenges, or they are
    specialised drills.
  - Their IDs stay reserved and are never reused (last section).

Nothing is timed, mandatory or a prerequisite. Learn recommends an order;
people (and teachers) choose.

## How to read an exercise

Each exercise has:

- a **stable ID**: never reused. Display order is separate. A merged
  exercise keeps the ID of its main original.
- an English and a Hebrew title.
- **Objective** and **Materials**.
- **Student:** the short card text. Hebrew is written during implementation,
  gender-neutral, and reviewed by a native speaker.
- **Guide:** shown behind "More help".
- **Reference**, **Mistake → fix**, **Tool** and **Worksheet**.
- **Success:** what the drawing shows, never a score.

"When overlaid" means: photograph the drawing and check it in Compare &
Correct. That is an optional self-check, not a test.

Tool abbreviations:

- **Measure** — Measure & Plan
- **Study** — Study & Simplify
- **Color** — Color & Mixing
- **Grid** — Create a Grid
- **Compare** — Compare & Correct
- **Worksheets** — Prepare Worksheets

## Core

**1 · D-START-01 · A first drawing from life · רישום ראשון מהתבוננות**
- Objective: draw a real object from observation before anything else, and keep it as a baseline.
- Materials: HB pencil, A4 paper, a mug or cup.
- Student: "Put a mug in front of you. Look at it more than at the paper, and draw what you see."
- Guide:
  - Look slowly, and don't erase yet.
  - Keep the drawing. Repeat this exercise after the core to see the change for yourself (no grading).
  - Optional warm-up: trace the mug's edge very slowly with your eyes on the mug, looking at the paper only to restart. This "slow contour" (Nicolaides) trains attention; it is untested as a way to draw more accurately (01 §2).
- Reference: a real object; a provided photo for anyone without one.
- Mistake → fix: drawing the idea of a mug (a round opening seen from above) → compare the opening's height with its width: from the side it is a flat ellipse.
- Tool: Compare (optional, with a photo of the same mug).
- Worksheet: no.
- Success: the outline, the opening and the handle are all there, in roughly the right places.

**2 · D-SHAPE-01 · Big shapes and simple forms · צורות גדולות וגופים פשוטים**
- Objective: see objects as one big flat shape first, then as a few simple 3-D forms, before any detail.
- Materials: soft pencil, paper, two or three household objects (a bottle, a cup, a box).
- Student: "Draw each object first as its outer shape only. Then build it from simple forms (box, cylinder, ball), and refine."
- Guide:
  - A filled silhouette shows proportion errors that outlines hide.
  - Simple forms on one shared axis keep the object solid.
  - A cylinder's two ends are both ellipses.
- Reference: real objects; provided photos as fallback.
- Mistake → fix: starting with details → squint until the details disappear, and draw what is left.
- Tool: Study (Values, 2–3 levels) to see the silhouette in a photo.
- Worksheet: no.
- Success: each object is recognisable from its silhouette alone and looks solid when built from forms.

**3 · D-LINE-04 · Lines and ellipses on a real object · קווים ואליפסות על חפץ אמיתי**
- Objective: just enough hand control for confident lines and ellipses, applied at once.
- Materials: pencil, paper, a glass or jar.
- Student: "Warm up with a few lines between two dots and a few ellipses, each in one stroke. Then draw a glass: straight sides, an ellipse for the rim, a curve for the base."
- Guide:
  - Long lines come from the elbow: move over the path first, then draw.
  - Below eye level, the base ellipse is a little rounder than the rim.
  - Keep the warm-up short: hand control is rarely what limits accuracy (01 §1).
- Reference: a real object (provided photo as fallback).
- Mistake → fix: a flat line for the base → the base is part of an ellipse too.
- Tool: Measure (optional).
- Worksheet: yes — a warm-up page of dot pairs and ellipse boxes.
- Success: the sides are straight, and the rim and base curve consistently.

**4 · D-OBS-02 · Negative space · חלל שלילי**
- Objective: draw the shapes around and between parts of an object to get its proportions right.
- Materials: pencil, paper, a chair or a plant.
- Student: "Draw only the empty shapes between the legs and bars of a chair. The chair appears by itself."
- Guide: Empty shapes have no name, so they are drawn as seen rather than as known.
- Reference: real subject; provided photo as fallback.
- Mistake → fix: drawing the parts from memory → compare the widths and heights of the gaps instead.
- Tool: Study (Values, 2 levels) makes the empty shapes obvious in a photo.
- Worksheet: optional — a high-contrast reference.
- Success: the gaps have believable sizes and the chair's proportions read correctly.

**5 · D-OBS-03 · Angles and alignments · זוויות וקווי יישור**
- Objective: judge angles against vertical and horizontal, and place points by what lines up.
- Materials: pencil, paper, a subject with clear edges (a chair, a window, a box).
- Student: "Hold the pencil at arm's length along an edge, keep its angle and lay it on the paper. Then find what lines up straight down or across, and draw light guide lines."
- Guide:
  - Keep the pencil flat in an imaginary window parallel to your face, with one eye closed.
  - Check every new point against two others.
  - Angle errors follow predictable biases of perception, so checking beats guessing (01 §1).
- Reference: real subject or a provided photo.
- Mistake → fix: tilting the pencil toward you → keep it in the window plane.
- Tool: Measure (angles and guide lines on a photo); Compare (optional).
- Worksheet: optional — a reference with a few alignments marked.
- Success: the main angles and alignments match the subject when checked.

**6 · D-OBS-04 · Relative measurement · מדידה יחסית בעזרת העיפרון**
- Objective: compare proportions against one chosen unit.
- Materials: pencil, paper, a bottle or any object.
- Student: "Pick one small part as your unit and measure everything else in units, with your arm straight and locked."
- Guide:
  - Measure the big relationships and check one against another (height against width).
  - Glance between subject and drawing often: frequent short checks go with more accurate drawings (01 §1).
- Reference: real subject or a provided photo.
- Mistake → fix: bending the arm between measurements → lock the elbow, so the scale stays the same.
- Tool: Measure (real proportions on a photo).
- Worksheet: no.
- Success: the main height-to-width relationships match when checked with Measure or Compare.

**7 · D-OBS-06 · Block-in: from the big shape to the small · חלוקה ראשונית: מהצורה הגדולה לקטנה**
- Objective: start any complex shape with a few straight lines, curves last.
- Materials: pencil, paper.
- Student: "Enclose the subject in four to eight straight lines, then cut smaller shapes inside them. Curves come last."
- Guide:
  - This is the atelier block-in (Bargue tradition).
  - Nothing smaller than a thumb until the big shape is right.
  - Copy a complex contour as short, simple segments, one at a time, as experienced drawers do (01 §1).
- Reference: provided reference (a shoe, a bird, a cast).
- Mistake → fix: details too early → stay with the big shape until it matches.
- Tool: Compare (photograph the block-in and overlay it).
- Worksheet: yes — reference and an empty frame of the same size.
- Success: when overlaid, the block-in's outer shape matches the reference's proportions.

**8 · D-COPY-02 · A measured copy, checked and corrected · העתקה מדויקת: בדיקה ותיקון**
- Objective: copy a simple image at the same size with block-in and measurement, then check it and correct it.
- Materials: pencil, the printed reference, paper.
- Student: "Copy the reference at the same size. Then check it, find the largest error, fix it, and check again."
- Guide:
  - Same-size copying (as with the Bargue plates) makes checking direct.
  - Feedback works best when it is about the drawing, never about the person (01 §2).
- Reference: a provided simple reference.
- Mistake → fix: correcting everything by feel → fix the largest error first.
- Tool: Compare.
- Worksheet: yes — reference and an empty frame of the same size.
- Success: when overlaid, the main shapes align closely after one round of correction.

**9 · D-VAL-01 · Seeing values · לראות ערכים**
- Objective: make an even value scale, then simplify a picture into three values.
- Materials: HB, 2B and 4B pencils, paper.
- Student: "Fill five boxes from white to your darkest dark in even steps. Then simplify a photo to three values in Study & Simplify, and draw those three shapes."
- Guide:
  - Value is lightness alone (Munsell's scale).
  - Build darks in layers with a softer pencil.
  - Squint to merge small shapes into the big shape they belong to.
  - Smooth transitions and hatching can be practised on the same strip.
- Reference: provided photo or own.
- Mistake → fix: middle steps that look alike, or copying every small spot → even steps; keep only the big shapes.
- Tool: Study (Values, 3 levels).
- Worksheet: yes — a 5-step strip, the 3-value version and an outline.
- Success: neighbouring steps are distinguishable, and the drawing reads with three tones only.

**10 · D-LIGHT-01 · Light and shadow on simple objects · אור וצל על חפצים פשוטים**
- Objective: light side, shadow side, core shadow, reflected light and cast shadow, on a ball, a box and a cup under one light.
- Materials: a ball, a box, a cup, one lamp, pencils.
- Student: "Light the objects from one side. Find where light turns to shadow and the shape of each cast shadow, then shade."
- Guide:
  - Each face of the box gets one value; the ball turns gradually.
  - Reflected light stays darker than any lit area.
  - Cast shadows are darkest and sharpest where they touch the object.
- Reference: real setup; provided photo as fallback.
- Mistake → fix: outlining everything in dark lines → let neighbouring values make the edges.
- Tool: Study (grayscale of a photo of the setup, optional).
- Worksheet: no.
- Success: one light direction on every object; light and shadow sides separate clearly; cast shadows point away from the light.

**11 · D-OBS-07 · A still life from life, start to finish · טבע דומם מהתבוננות, מההתחלה עד הסוף**
- Objective: placement, measurement, block-in and light in one drawing of two or three objects.
- Materials: pencils, paper, two or three objects, one lamp.
- Student: "Mark the group's top, bottom and sides, block in each object, map the shadow shapes, then shade."
- Guide: Work the whole drawing at once rather than finishing one object at a time.
- Reference: real objects.
- Mistake → fix: drawing too small or off the page → mark the extremes before anything else.
- Tool: Measure; Study; Compare.
- Worksheet: optional.
- Success: the group fits the page as planned, relative sizes read correctly, and the light is consistent.

**12 · D-HEAD-04 · A portrait from a photo · דיוקן מתצלום**
- Objective: a portrait measured first, then checked against average proportions.
- Materials: pencils, worksheets.
- Student: "Block in the head, place the features by measuring and aligning, check them against the average map, then shade in three values."
- Guide:
  - The eyes are usually near the middle of the head's height.
  - But real faces vary, and the classical canons rarely fit them.
  - Knowing the average only partly fixes the habit of drawing the eyes too high (01 §3). Measure first, check second.
- Reference: provided portrait, or own photo with the sitter's consent.
- Mistake → fix: eyes too high and a small forehead → measure the eye line against the head's height.
- Tool: Measure; Study; Grid; Compare; Worksheets.
- Worksheet: yes — reference, outline, grid, values, average proportion map.
- Success: when overlaid, the main features align closely.

## Next steps

**13 · D-COPY-01 · The grid method · שיטת הרשת**
- Objective: copy an image accurately square by square, and understand proportional transfer.
- Materials: pencil, the printed worksheets, ruler.
- Student: "Copy one square at a time: look at where the edges enter and leave each square."
- Guide:
  - Start with 4 × 4, and go finer for detail.
  - The grid helps transfer and check; measuring stays the skill.
- Reference: provided reference or own photo.
- Mistake → fix: drawing the subject instead of the square → treat each square as an abstract shape.
- Tool: Grid; Worksheets.
- Worksheet: yes — reference with grid, matching blank grid.
- Success: when overlaid, the main contours fall in the right squares.

**14 · D-PERSP-01 · Eye level and perspective from observation · קו האופק ופרספקטיבה מהתבוננות**
- Objective: find eye level, and use converging lines (one and two vanishing points) to check drawings of real rooms, boxes and streets.
- Materials: pencil, paper, ruler (optional).
- Student: "Find your eye level first. Draw a corner of a room or a few boxes: lines going away from you meet on the eye-level line. Check the angles with your pencil."
- Guide:
  - Perspective explains what you measure; measuring still leads.
  - Vanishing points far apart (even off the page) avoid distortion.
- Reference: real scene or own photo.
- Mistake → fix: a horizon placed anywhere → it is always your eye level.
- Tool: Measure (angles on a photo); Compare.
- Worksheet: yes — eye-level and vanishing-point guides.
- Success: receding edges converge on the eye-level line and agree with the measured angles.

**15 · D-FIG-01 · Gesture and figure proportions · תנועה ופרופורציות הגוף**
- Objective: capture movement first, then check proportions in head lengths.
- Materials: pencil, paper.
- Student: "Draw people or poses with a few flowing lines that show the movement. Then, on one standing figure, mark chin, chest, navel, hips, knees and feet in head lengths."
- Guide:
  - Many small gesture drawings, no details, no timer.
  - The adult average is about 7.5 heads (8 is the idealised figure); children have fewer (01 §3).
  - Measure the real figure; the head count is a check.
- Reference: real people or provided pose photos.
- Mistake → fix:
  - stiff outlines → start with one curve through the whole body;
  - legs too short → for adults, the hips are near half the height.
- Tool: Measure; Worksheets.
- Worksheet: yes — head-unit guide.
- Success: each gesture shows the pose's direction and balance, and the standing figure's landmarks fall near their expected places.

**16 · D-FIG-04 · Hands · ידיים**
- Objective: the hand's structure: the palm as a box, finger segments, the base of the thumb.
- Materials: pencil, paper, your free hand.
- Student: "Draw your free hand: the palm as a box first, then the finger blocks, then the outline."
- Guide: Finger segments shorten toward the tips, and the knuckles form a curve.
- Reference: own hand; provided hand photos.
- Mistake → fix: fingers as equal sausages → segments that shorten toward the tips.
- Tool: none.
- Worksheet: no.
- Success: palm and fingers have believable proportions and joints.

**17 · D-COPY-03 · Constructing and shading an eye · בניית עין והצללתה**
- Objective: the eye as a form, then in values.
- Materials: pencils, paper, worksheets.
- Student: "Start with a ball, wrap the lids around it, and place the iris partly under the upper lid. Then shade in three values, keeping only the highlight paper-white."
- Guide:
  - The upper lid casts a shadow on the eyeball.
  - Lashes curve and clump.
  - The white of the eye is shaded.
- Reference: provided eye photos (front and three-quarter).
- Mistake → fix: the whole iris showing (a staring look) → let the upper lid cover its top.
- Tool: Study (Values); Worksheets.
- Worksheet: yes — reference, outline, grid, values.
- Success: the eye reads as a form with lid thickness, and the highlight is the brightest spot.

**18 · D-HEAD-02 · The head turning · הראש בסיבוב: שלושה רבעים ופרופיל**
- Objective: the head as a ball and jaw turning in space (Loomis-style construction).
- Materials: pencil, paper.
- Student: "Draw the head as a ball with a flat side, add the jaw and neck, then turn it to three-quarter and profile."
- Guide: The centre line curves with the turn, and the far eye narrows.
- Reference: provided three-quarter and profile photos.
- Mistake → fix: features staying frontal while the head turns → wrap them along the curved centre line.
- Tool: none.
- Worksheet: optional.
- Success: the features wrap around the head consistently with the turn.

**19 · D-SUBJ-02 · Landscape: big shapes and depth · נוף: צורות גדולות ועומק**
- Objective: simplify a landscape into a few planes and show depth (less contrast and lighter values with distance).
- Materials: pencils, paper.
- Student: "Divide the scene into foreground, middle ground, background and sky, and give each its own value."
- Guide: Atmospheric perspective: distant shapes lose contrast and detail.
- Reference: own photo or a provided landscape.
- Mistake → fix: equal detail everywhere → keep detail and contrast for the foreground.
- Tool: Study (Values).
- Worksheet: optional.
- Success: the planes separate clearly and recede.

**20 · D-SUBJ-04 · A complete drawing · רישום שלם מההתחלה עד הסוף**
- Objective: from a few value thumbnails to one finished drawing.
- Materials: pencils, paper, worksheets.
- Student: "Make two or three palm-sized value sketches and choose the clearest. Then block in, shade and finish one drawing."
- Guide:
  - Guidelines such as the rule of thirds are options, not laws.
  - A full value pass comes before any detail.
- Reference: own photo or provided.
- Mistake → fix: rushing to details → finish the big values first.
- Tool: Worksheets; Study; Measure; Compare.
- Worksheet: yes.
- Success: a planned composition with a clear focal point, believable proportions and consistent light.

## Colour module (separate; pencil first recommended, never required)

**C-COL-01 · Hue, value and chroma · גוון, ערך ורוויה**
- Objective: the three dimensions of colour, and why value carries the form.
- Materials: paints (acrylic, gouache or watercolour), brush, paper.
- Student: "Paint one colour from light to dark, then swatches of different colours that look equally light."
- Guide: Check value with a grayscale photo of the swatches.
- Reference: none.
- Mistake → fix: judging lightness by hue → check in grayscale.
- Tool: Color; Study (grayscale).
- Worksheet: optional — swatch boxes.
- Success: swatches meant to share a value look alike in grayscale.

**C-COL-02 · Mixing with a limited palette · ערבוב בפלטה מצומצמת**
- Objective: mix a chart from a few colours and match a target colour.
- Materials: a warm and a cool version of each primary, white, palette, paper.
- Student: "Mix a small chart from your colours, then mix one colour from a photo as closely as you can."
- Guide: Fewer tubes means more control over mixtures.
- Reference: a provided or own photo.
- Mistake → fix: black to darken everything → darken with darker or complementary colours.
- Tool: Color (target colour and mixing guidance).
- Worksheet: optional — chart boxes.
- Success: a full chart, and one target matched by eye.

**C-COL-03 · Preparing a canvas · הכנת קנבס**
- Objective: choose size and ratio, prime the surface, optionally tone it.
- Materials: canvas or board, gesso, brush.
- Student: "Choose a canvas that matches your reference's ratio, prime it evenly, and tone it if you like."
- Guide: A mid-value toned ground makes judging lights and darks easier.
- Reference: none.
- Mistake → fix: a ratio that does not match the reference → plan the ratio first.
- Tool: Grid (canvas size and ratio).
- Worksheet: no.
- Success: an evenly primed surface in the planned ratio.

**C-COL-04 · Transferring the drawing · העברת הרישום לקנבס**
- Objective: transfer a drawing to the canvas accurately.
- Materials: canvas, pencil or thin paint, ruler.
- Student: "Draw a light grid on the canvas matching your printed grid, and transfer square by square."
- Guide: Keep transfer lines light so they do not muddy the paint.
- Reference: own reference with its worksheets.
- Mistake → fix: heavy graphite lines → light lines, or redraw them in thin paint.
- Tool: Grid; Measure; Worksheets.
- Worksheet: yes — reference with grid, blank grid.
- Success: the main contours are in the right place on the canvas.

**C-COL-05 · Simplifying into value and colour groups · פישוט לקבוצות ערך וצבע**
- Objective: plan a painting with three to five values and a few colour groups.
- Materials: paints, small paper or board.
- Student: "Simplify the reference to three to five values and a few colours, and paint a small study."
- Guide: The study is a plan, not a finished painting.
- Reference: own or provided photo.
- Mistake → fix: too many small colour shapes → merge them into the big groups.
- Tool: Study (Values; Color and Painter modes).
- Worksheet: optional — value version.
- Success: the study reads clearly at a distance.

**C-COL-06 · Block-in and layers · מסות ראשונות ושכבות**
- Objective: big shapes first in thin paint, then refine toward detail in layers.
- Materials: paints, large and small brushes, the prepared canvas.
- Student: "Cover the canvas with big shapes in thin paint first; refine and add detail in later layers."
- Guide: With acrylics, let layers dry; with oils, follow "fat over lean".
- Reference: own reference.
- Mistake → fix: detail before the canvas is covered → big shapes first, everywhere.
- Tool: Compare (check progress against the reference).
- Worksheet: no.
- Success: the whole canvas is covered with big shapes before any detail.

**C-COL-07 · A small complete painting · ציור קטן ושלם**
- Objective: one small painting from reference to finish.
- Materials: paints, canvas or board.
- Student: "Plan, transfer, block in and finish one small painting."
- Guide: Reuse the earlier colour exercises as steps.
- Reference: own or provided.
- Mistake → fix: losing the value plan while adding colour → check the painting in grayscale.
- Tool: Worksheets; Grid; Study; Color; Compare.
- Worksheet: yes.
- Success: a finished small painting whose value structure matches its plan.
